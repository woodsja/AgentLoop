## Context
Issue #172 requires one explicit milestone authorization followed by unattended release of two initially planned and one subsequently planned synthetic ticket. The current documented contract in docs/milestone-grants.md deliberately requires owner confirmation of each exact ticket: src/milestone-batches.js delegates each approval to src/milestone-tickets.js. Grant categories, a planner's scope declaration and one model verdict cannot establish semantic permission. No accepted ADR chooses how an owner may delegate authorization for later ticket bytes. Choosing that authority boundary is a prerequisite to implementation, and #172 explicitly identifies itself as planning/owner-decision required. The choice changes durable authorization evidence and restart/admission/merge semantics, so it cannot be treated as a routine prompt removal.

## Alternatives and trade-offs
- Owner-approved immutable ticket manifest: Bind the initial grant authorization to the complete exact content and dependencies of a finite ticket manifest. Release matching tickets unattended while preserving exact receipts, quota and lifecycle checks. This provides a simple, auditable proof of scope without semantic inference, but a genuinely new later ticket requires a separately approved manifest/grant revision. It therefore does not satisfy the subsequently planned ticket acceptance case unless that ticket's exact content was already approved; delaying publication alone must not be presented as later planning.
- Owner-approved bounded work contracts with deterministic refinement: At grant authorization, approve finite work contracts and explicit, machine-checkable rules for generating or refining tickets, including permitted content/templates, dependencies, exclusions and per-contract quotas. Initial and later tickets receive exact receipts only when a deterministic verifier can bind their complete executable content to those contracts; free prose, self-asserted categories and model verdicts cannot supply missing authority. This can support later planning within approved contracts, but limits planning flexibility and requires a precisely specified verifier and durable proof format. Uncovered or ambiguous content must remain held for explicit owner review. A generic objective in natural language is insufficient.
- Retain exact-ticket owner confirmation: Preserve the existing documented authority model and receipt format, optionally consolidating review of already known exact tickets into one explicit batch confirmation. This minimizes migration and semantic-verification risk, but cannot authorize genuinely new later ticket scope unattended and leaves the goal-level product acceptance unmet.

## Decision
Owner-approved immutable ticket manifest
The owner explicitly chose one approval for the complete fixed set of exact ticket identities, contents and dependencies. A subsequently added or changed ticket is not part of that approved batch and needs a new owner decision. One approval must cover the grant and the immutable batch; preserve individual receipts and fail-closed scope verification.

## Accepted trade-offs
Bind the initial grant authorization to the complete exact content and dependencies of a finite ticket manifest. Release matching tickets unattended while preserving exact receipts, quota and lifecycle checks. This provides a simple, auditable proof of scope without semantic inference, but a genuinely new later ticket requires a separately approved manifest/grant revision. It therefore does not satisfy the subsequently planned ticket acceptance case unless that ticket's exact content was already approved; delaying publication alone must not be presented as later planning.

## Consequences
The chosen model must bind authorization to exact repository, specification bytes/digest, grant digest/revision and immutable ticket content, with dependency and issued/active quota constraints. Any delegated model needs durable proof linking each exact receipt to the owner's approved bounds, retained with complete raw owner evidence across fresh processes and failures. Existing grants and receipts must retain their original authority semantics; no automatic conversion from per-ticket approval to delegated authority. Pause, revoke, amendment/edit, expiry and changed ticket/native dependency evidence must invalidate authority at existing pre-work and pre-merge gates. Preserve opt-out projects and the existing stores, queue and scheduler. After a separately approved design, demonstrate one grant confirmation and unattended release of two initial plus one genuinely later planned qualifying ticket, denial of a ready-labeled outsider and ambiguous/over-boundary candidates, restart recovery and lifecycle invalidation using red-capable public-boundary tests. Full check, independent review, exact-head CI and real-host synthetic smoke remain required before enabling Step 12; none has been established by this proposal.

## Existing ADR conflicts
ADR 0001 remains accepted and is not reopened: preserve complete owner-visible captured evidence, ordinary disposable-VM execution and the physical-host boundary. The delegated alternatives would intentionally replace the exact-ticket owner-only scope policy documented in docs/milestone-grants.md and enforced by milestone-tickets.js; that policy change requires an explicit owner decision. docs/github-issue-tracker.md still separates unlabeled draft publication from executable release. Issue #172's planning-only status prohibits treating the task wording or this proposal as approval of a new permission model. No other accepted ADR was found in docs/decisions selecting delegated scope authority.
Resolution: The original issue #172 was a planning-only exploration that offered dynamic later planning as an alternative. The owner later chose a fixed immutable manifest and explicitly rejected automatic expansion. That decision supersedes the former subsequently-planned-ticket acceptance criterion. Preserve accepted ADR 0001 and its host boundary/owner observability, and preserve old per-ticket scope authority for prior grants; the new fixed-batch mode must be separate and explicit. No existing accepted ADR is superseded.
